# Trade Grow — Cold Call Playbook

**Audience:** Sales executives, team leaders, QA and compliance
**Status:** Draft for Compliance sign-off before use
**Rule:** Nothing in this playbook may be used until Compliance has approved it and the
placeholders (registration number, charges, timelines) have been filled from verified config.

---

## 0. The governing principle

We are a new platform. Our sales strategy is not *"trust us."* It is:

> **Verify us. Compare us. Understand us. Then decide.**

Every script below exists to move the customer toward independent verification, not away from it.
An agent who talks a sceptical customer *out of* checking us has damaged the business, even if they
book the account.

### Why this actually converts better for us

A customer who has checked our SEBI number, compared our charges and read our FAQ before opening an
account is dramatically more likely to fund it and trade. A customer talked into KYC on a call is
likely to abandon at e-sign or never fund. **Activated, funded clients are the target — not KYC starts.**

---

## 1. Absolute prohibitions

These are not style guidance. Breaching any of these is a terminable offence and, in several cases,
a regulatory violation.

| # | Never | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask for OTP, password, PIN, UPI PIN, card PIN or net banking credentials | Fraud. No exceptions, ever, for any reason |
| 2 | Offer, imply or hint at assured returns, guaranteed profits or fixed income | Prohibited by regulation |
| 3 | Give buy/sell recommendations, tips, targets or stock calls | We are not an advisor and hold no advisory registration |
| 4 | Claim a SEBI registration, exchange membership or depository status not published on `/verify` | Misrepresentation of regulatory status |
| 5 | Say "zero tax", "tax-free trading" or "no charges at all" | Statutory charges apply on every trade by law |
| 6 | Complete a customer's KYC form for them, or use their credentials | Regulatory breach and fraud exposure |
| 7 | Ask a customer to install a screen-sharing or remote-access app | Standard fraud vector |
| 8 | Ask for a transfer to any personal account, UPI ID or wallet | Funds move only through official platform channels |
| 9 | Call a number on the DND/NDNC list or one that has asked not to be contacted | Legal requirement |
| 10 | Disparage a competitor, or claim they are unsafe/hiding charges | Legally risky and commercially pointless |
| 11 | Create urgency that does not exist ("offer closes tonight") | Prohibited sales practice |
| 12 | Continue after a clear refusal | Mark DND and move on |

**If a customer volunteers an OTP:** stop them immediately. *"Sir, please don't share that with me
or anyone. I don't need it and I'm not able to take it. Please enter it only on your own screen."*
Log the incident.

## 2. Call structure (every call, every time)

```
1. IDENTIFY      Who you are, which company, why you're calling          ~15 sec
2. PERMISSION    Confirm it's an okay time                               ~10 sec
3. DISCOVER      Current broker, frequency, main frustration             ~60 sec
4. RELEVANCE     Address the ONE thing they raised, not the full pitch   ~45 sec
5. VERIFY        Offer verification details, unprompted                  ~30 sec
6. HANDOFF       WhatsApp the trust pack, confirm receipt                ~20 sec
7. NEXT STEP     Agree a specific follow-up, or close politely
```

Target talk/listen ratio: **40/60**. If you are talking more than the customer in the discovery
phase, you are pitching, not qualifying.

**Never skip step 5.** Offering verification before being asked is the single highest-trust moment
available to a new broker. It is worth more than any feature you can describe.

## 3. Opening scripts (5 versions)

> `[AGENT]` = your name. `[REG]` = registration number from `/verify` — **only if published**.
> If registration details are still pending, use Opening 5 and the disclosure in §3.6.

### 3.1 Opening A — Standard / neutral

> "Good morning Sir/Ma'am, my name is **[AGENT]**, I'm calling from Trade Grow.
> We're a new digital trading platform, and we're focused on transparent pricing and a simple
> account-opening process.
>
> I want to be upfront — I'm not calling to ask you for any money today. What I'd like to do first
> is send you our official company, regulatory and pricing details so you can check them yourself.
>
> Just so I send the right information — are you currently using any Demat account?"

### 3.2 Opening B — Cost-led (for known active traders)

> "Good afternoon Sir/Ma'am, this is **[AGENT]** from Trade Grow.
>
> We're a new trading platform, and the one thing we do differently is that we break out every
> charge separately — brokerage on one line, and the statutory charges like STT and GST on their
> own lines, so you can see exactly where your money goes.
>
> I'm not asking you to switch anything today. May I ask — when you place a trade currently, do you
> actually check the charge breakdown on your contract note, or is it mostly just the brokerage
> you look at?"

### 3.3 Opening C — Referral or inbound follow-up

> "Hello Sir/Ma'am, this is **[AGENT]** from Trade Grow. You had **[filled a form / been referred
> by X / enquired on our website]** about opening a trading account — is now a convenient time to
> speak for two minutes?
>
> …Thank you. Before I explain anything about our platform, I'd like to send you our verification
> page — that's our company details, SEBI registration and charges — so that you're checking us
> rather than taking my word for it. Is this number on WhatsApp?"

### 3.4 Opening D — Very short / busy-sounding customer

> "Sir/Ma'am, **[AGENT]** from Trade Grow, I'll be thirty seconds.
>
> We're a new trading platform. I'd like to WhatsApp you our official details and charges so you
> can look at them whenever you have time — no call needed, no commitment.
>
> Shall I send it to this number?"

*This is the highest-yield opening for cold lists. It asks for almost nothing and ends with a
single yes/no question.*

### 3.5 Opening E — Pre-launch / registration details still pending

Use this when `/verify` still shows pending fields. **Do not use A–D in that state.**

> "Good morning Sir/Ma'am, this is **[AGENT]** from Trade Grow.
>
> We're a new trading platform currently in our launch phase. I want to be straightforward with you
> — some of our regulatory details are still being finalised and published, and I'm not going to
> claim anything that isn't up on our website yet.
>
> What I'd like to do is send you our details page so you can see exactly where we stand today, and
> follow up with you once everything is published. Would that be alright?"

### 3.6 Mandatory disclosure while details are pending

If any registration field on `/verify` is pending and the customer asks about registration, the
agent **must** say:

> "I want to be clear with you — that detail isn't published on our website yet, which means I'm not
> in a position to confirm it on this call. I'd rather tell you that than give you a number you
> can't check. I'll let you know as soon as it's up, and you'll be able to verify it yourself in
> SEBI's own database."

## 4. Discovery questions

Ask three to five. Listen to the answers. Do not run them as a checklist.

| Goal | Question |
|---|---|
| Current provider | "Which platform are you using at the moment?" |
| Frequency | "Roughly how often do you trade — daily, weekly, or more long-term investing?" |
| Segment | "Mostly delivery, or do you do intraday and F&O as well?" |
| Pain | "Is there anything about your current platform that genuinely annoys you?" |
| Cost awareness | "Do you have a sense of what a typical trade actually costs you, all charges included?" |
| Decision style | "When you picked your current broker, what made you go with them?" |
| Objection surfacing | "What would make you hesitant about trying a newer platform?" |

**The last one is deliberate.** Surfacing the objection yourself is far stronger than waiting for it.

## 5. Objection scripts

### 5.1 "Your platform is new. How can I trust you?" — *the core objection*

**Master response:**

> "That's a completely fair question, and honestly you shouldn't trust us just because I'm calling
> you. That's not a good reason to trust anyone.
>
> What I'll do instead is send you our verification page. It has our legal entity name, our
> registration details and our compliance officer's contact. And every one of those links to
> SEBI's own database or the exchange's own directory — places we don't control and can't edit.
>
> Check us there. Compare our charges with what you're paying now. If it looks suitable to you,
> then you can decide about opening an account. And if it doesn't, that's a perfectly fine outcome
> too."

**Ten variations** — same substance, different register:

1. **Short:** "Fair question. Don't trust me — check us. I'll send you our SEBI details and you can look them up in SEBI's own database. Then decide."
2. **Empathetic:** "I'd ask the same thing, honestly. Money and KYC documents aren't things to hand over casually. So let me send you what you'd need to check us out, and take whatever time you want with it."
3. **Direct:** "You shouldn't trust us yet. We haven't earned it. What we've done instead is publish everything you'd need to verify us independently — that's the most a new company can honestly offer."
4. **Comparative:** "Being new is a real disadvantage and I won't pretend otherwise. What I can tell you is that a registered broker is checkable in exactly the same way whether it's twenty years old or two months old. Let me show you how to check us."
5. **Process-led:** "Here's what I'd suggest. Don't decide anything on this call. I'll send you three things — our registration details, our full charge list, and our FAQ. Read them, and I'll call back in two days for your questions."
6. **Regulatory:** "The thing worth understanding is that your shares sit in your own demat account with CDSL or NSDL — not with us. Any broker should be able to explain that clearly. If one can't, that tells you something."
7. **Reversal:** "Genuinely, what would you need to see to feel comfortable? If it's something we can publish, I'd like to know, because we'd rather publish it than argue about it."
8. **Transparent-about-limits:** "I'll be honest — we can't give you a ten-year track record, because we don't have one. What we can give you is the regulatory position and the pricing, both in writing. The rest you'd be judging on trust, and I'd rather you judged on documents."
9. **No-pressure:** "There's no rush from our side. Keep your existing account, look at our details whenever you have time, and if you ever want to try us, we'll be here. Nothing expires."
10. **For a repeat sceptic:** "You've been careful about this, which is exactly right. Can I ask what specifically you'd want to verify? I'll point you at the source for that one thing rather than sending you a pile of links."

### 5.2 "Is it SEBI registered?"

**If published:**
> "Yes — our registration number is **[REG]**, and it's on our website at tradegrow.in/verify.
> More usefully, you can search that number directly on SEBI's own intermediaries database and
> confirm the entity name matches. I'd genuinely encourage you to do that rather than take my word."

**If pending:** use §3.6 verbatim. **Do not improvise here.**

### 5.3 "Is my money safe?"

> "Let me answer that precisely rather than just saying yes.
>
> Your shares sit in a demat account with CDSL or NSDL, in your own name — not with the broker. You
> can check your holdings with the depository directly, independent of us.
>
> Funds move only between the platform and a bank account in your own name. We can't send money
> anywhere else, and we're not permitted to.
>
> What I won't tell you is that there's no risk at all. Market risk is entirely yours, and any
> platform is a business that can have problems. Anyone telling you a trading account is completely
> safe isn't being straight with you."

### 5.4 "I already use Zerodha / Groww / Angel One / Upstox"

> "That's completely fine — they're established platforms with large customer bases, and there's
> nothing wrong with your choice.
>
> You don't need to close anything. You can hold accounts with more than one broker, it's entirely
> normal. So you could look at Trade Grow separately and just see whether the pricing or the
> platform is useful for some of your future trades.
>
> May I send you our charge list so you can compare it against what you're paying? If we don't come
> out better for how you actually trade, that's genuinely useful for you to know."

**Never:** say a competitor is expensive, unsafe, hiding charges, or about to have problems.

### 5.5 "Send details on WhatsApp"

Treat as a **positive** outcome, not a brush-off.

> "Of course — is this number on WhatsApp? … I'll send you four things: our verification page, our
> full charge list, a look at the platform, and our FAQ.
>
> One request — when it arrives, just open the verification page. It takes about two minutes and
> it's the part that actually matters. I'll follow up on **[specific day]** to see if you have
> questions. Is that alright?"

→ Set lead stage `WHATSAPP SENT`, log follow-up date. Send within 5 minutes of the call ending.

### 5.6 "I'll think about it"

> "Absolutely, please do. Can I ask — is there something specific you want to check, or is it more
> that you'd like time to look at it properly?"

- *Specific concern:* → handle it, then send the matching page.
- *Wants time:* → "That's sensible. I'll send everything across now, and check back on **[day]**. If you'd rather I didn't call, just tell me and I won't."

### 5.7 "Call me later"

> "No problem. What time usually works better for you — morning or evening? … I'll call you
> **[day, time]** then. And I'll send the details on WhatsApp now so you have them in the meantime."

→ Log exact callback time. **Honour it.** Missing an agreed callback is the fastest way to confirm
a customer's suspicion that we are just another call centre.

### 5.8 "Not interested"

> "That's completely fine, thank you for your time. If it's useful, I'll send our details once so
> you have them if anything changes — or I can leave it entirely, whichever you prefer."

- Accepts → send once, mark `NOT INTERESTED`, no further calls.
- Declines → mark `NOT INTERESTED`, **no message at all**.
- Says stop calling → mark `DND` immediately. This is a legal obligation, not a preference.

**One de-escalation attempt maximum. Never a second.**

### 5.9 "What are your charges?"

> "I'll give you the honest version, which has two parts.
>
> Brokerage is the part we set — that's **[current published rate, or: 'being finalised and will be
> published before we go live']**.
>
> The second part is statutory charges — STT, GST, stamp duty, exchange and SEBI charges, and DP
> charges. Those are set by the government and the exchanges. Every broker in India charges them,
> they're identical everywhere, and nobody can waive them.
>
> So when you hear 'zero brokerage' anywhere, it means the first part only. I'd rather explain that
> now than have you find out from a contract note. Our website has a calculator where you can put
> in a real trade and see the full breakdown."

### 5.10 "Someone told me there's zero brokerage and zero tax"

> "Zero brokerage can be genuine — it's the broker's own charge, so a broker can set it to zero
> under a plan, usually with conditions attached about which segments and for how long.
>
> Zero tax isn't a real thing. Taxes on trades are levied by law and no broker can waive them.
> If someone told you that in our name, I'd genuinely like to know, because they're not authorised
> to say it and we'd want to act on it."

### 5.11 "What's the benefit of switching?"

> "Let me answer that for your situation rather than in general. You said you mainly do
> **[delivery / intraday / F&O]**, around **[frequency]**.
>
> For that pattern, the charges that matter most are **[relevant charges]**. Our rate on those is
> **[published rate or 'to be published']**.
>
> The honest answer is: the benefit is whatever the difference works out to for your actual volume.
> Put a real trade into our calculator and compare it. If the number isn't meaningful for you,
> switching probably isn't worth your time — and I'd rather tell you that than oversell it."

### 5.12 "How does KYC work / how long does it take?"

> "It's fully digital. Mobile number and OTP, PAN, your KYC details, bank account, then an
> Aadhaar-based e-sign. The whole thing is done on your own phone.
>
> The one thing I want to be clear about: every OTP in that process is entered by you, on your own
> screen. I will never ask you for it, and if anyone ever does — from us or from anyone — that's
> fraud.
>
> On timing, **[published TAT, or: 'I don't want to quote you a turnaround time we haven't tested
> yet. It'll be published on our website before we launch.']**"

### 5.13 "How do I withdraw my money?"

> "You place a withdrawal request in the app, and it goes only to the bank account registered in
> your own name. It can't go anywhere else — that's a regulatory rule, not our policy.
>
> The timeline follows the exchange settlement cycle, so funds from a recent sale take a little
> time to become withdrawable. The app shows your withdrawable balance separately from your total
> balance so there's no confusion.
>
> If you ever find a withdrawal isn't processed on the stated timeline, that's a grievance and
> there's an escalation path for it that goes past us to the exchange and SEBI."

### 5.14 "How secure is it? / Is my data safe?"

> "I'll tell you what's actually in place rather than using words like bank-grade, which don't mean
> anything specific.
>
> **[List only controls confirmed live in config.]**
>
> And the most important part is on your side: nobody from Trade Grow will ever ask you for an OTP,
> password or PIN. Most account fraud in India doesn't involve breaking into a broker — it involves
> persuading the customer to hand over a code. Our security awareness page lists the exact warning
> signs, and it's worth reading even if you never open an account with us."

### 5.15 "I'm a new investor, I don't know anything about this"

> "That's completely fine, and honestly it's a good reason to go slowly.
>
> Can I suggest something? Before thinking about any platform including ours, have a look at the
> guides on our website — what a demat account is, what the charges mean, how KYC works. They're
> plain-language and they don't push you toward us.
>
> Once that makes sense to you, you'll be in a much better position to compare platforms. I'm happy
> to answer questions any time, and there's no rush at all."

**Never** pressure a first-time investor into F&O or same-call KYC. Note this in the CRM.

### 5.16 "Can I keep my existing account?"

> "Yes, absolutely. There's no requirement to close anything, and holding accounts with more than
> one broker is completely normal. Plenty of people keep a long-term account in one place and use
> another for something else."

### 5.17 "Who are you people? I've never heard of Trade Grow"

> "That's fair — we're new, so you wouldn't have.
>
> Our legal entity is **[entity name or: 'published on our website's verify page']**, and everything
> about us is on tradegrow.in/verify, including our compliance officer's name and contact.
>
> I'd actually suggest you don't take my word for any of it. Look us up on SEBI's own site and on
> the exchange member directory — that's the check that means something."

### 5.18 "Do you give tips / which stock should I buy?"

> "We don't, and I'm not permitted to. Trade Grow is a platform for placing trades and seeing your
> costs and holdings — we don't give recommendations, tips or targets, and we hold no advisory
> registration.
>
> I'd also say this generally: be careful with anyone who does offer that over a phone call or a
> Telegram group. It's a very common way people lose money."

### 5.19 Customer is angry / has been scammed before

> "I'm sorry that happened to you. That's unfortunately common and it's not your fault.
>
> I'm not going to try and sell you anything on this call. If it's useful, our website has a page
> on exactly how these frauds work and how to check whether any platform is genuine — including us.
> Would it be alright if I sent you that one link, and nothing else?"

→ Mark the lead as `FOLLOW-UP` with a long horizon, or `NOT INTERESTED`. **Do not push.**

### 5.20 "How do I add funds / place an order?"

> "Funds go in through the Funds section of the app, from your own bank account — UPI or net
> banking. Never to anyone's personal account or UPI ID; that's not how it works and if anyone asks
> you to do that, it's a fraud.
>
> For orders: pick the stock, buy or sell, choose intraday or delivery, enter quantity, confirm.
> The order screen shows your estimated charges before you confirm, which is something we
> deliberately built in. There's a walkthrough of all the screens on our platform page — I can send
> that."

## 6. Trust-first sales process (10 steps — train every agent on this)

| Step | Action | What "done" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understand their current broker | You can name it in the CRM |
| 2 | Understand trading frequency and segment | Logged: delivery/intraday/F&O, frequency |
| 3 | Understand their main concern | One sentence in the CRM, in their words |
| 4 | Explain the *relevant* Trade Grow feature | One feature, matched to step 3 |
| 5 | Provide verification information | `/verify` link sent, unprompted |
| 6 | Allow independent verification | You did **not** rush them past it |
| 7 | Answer questions honestly, including "I don't know" | No invented answers |
| 8 | Assist with official onboarding | They complete it themselves |
| 9 | Never request OTP/password | Non-negotiable |
| 10 | Follow up after activation | Funded? First trade? Any problem? |

**"I don't know, I'll find out and call you back" is an approved answer and is expected to be used.**
Fabricating an answer is a disciplinary matter.

## 7. Call quality scorecard

QA samples a minimum of 5 calls per agent per week.

| Criterion | Weight | Fail condition |
|---|---|---|
| Identified self and company within 15s | 10 | Not stated |
| Confirmed convenient time | 5 | Not asked |
| Discovery before pitch | 15 | Pitched before understanding |
| Talk/listen ratio ≤ 50/50 | 10 | Agent dominated |
| Offered verification unprompted | 20 | Not offered |
| No prohibited claim (§1) | **Auto-fail** | Any breach |
| Handled objection without pressure | 15 | Pressure applied |
| Agreed a specific next step | 10 | Vague or none |
| CRM logged accurately within 10 min | 15 | Missing or wrong |

**Any §1 breach = automatic zero, immediate escalation to Compliance, regardless of other scores.**

## 8. Compensation guardrail

Commission must be tied to **activated + funded + first-traded** clients, not to KYC starts.

Paying on KYC starts creates exactly the incentive that produces pressure selling, abandoned
applications and compliance breaches. This is a business-design decision, not an HR detail.

Recommended: **clawback on any account that files a mis-selling complaint** within 90 days.

## 9. Daily agent routine

**Before calling**
- Check `/verify` for any newly published detail
- Read the day's script updates in the CRM script library
- Review follow-ups due today — these come before new leads

**During**
- Log within 10 minutes of each call, not at day end
- Record objection verbatim where possible
- Send the WhatsApp pack within 5 minutes of the call

**End of day**
- All calls dispositioned, no lead left in `NEW` after contact
- Every promised callback has a scheduled date
- Flag to the team leader: any customer who reported a suspicious call in our name

## 10. Script library structure (for the CRM)

Categories to implement as a searchable, admin-editable library:

`OPENING` · `TRUST` · `PRICING` · `KYC` · `SECURITY` · `EXISTING BROKER` · `COMPETITOR` ·
`FOLLOW-UP` · `CLOSING` · `NOT INTERESTED` · `CALL BACK` · `WHATSAPP`

Each script record: `id`, `category`, `title`, `body`, `version`, `approved_by`, `approved_on`,
`status (draft|approved|retired)`, `usage_count`, `linked_objection`.

- Agents can **search and copy**, not edit.
- Admins can edit; every edit creates an audit log entry and a new version.
- A script cannot move to `approved` without a Compliance user in `approved_by`.
